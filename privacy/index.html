Privacy policy

Curmudgeon Works · applies to Curmudgeon Keyboard · last updated 13 September 2026

The short version

Curmudgeon Keyboard does not collect, store, or transmit what you type. It has no analytics, no advertising, and no accounts.

What stays on your device

The keyboard keeps its settings, your personal dictionary, and the words it learns from your typing in the app's private storage on your phone. This data never leaves the device unless you export it yourself using the backup feature, which writes a file where you choose to save it.

Network access

The keyboard does not request the Internet permission. It cannot send data anywhere. Links inside the settings, such as to this website, open in your browser.

Permissions

As an input method the app needs the system permission to act as a keyboard, which Android grants when you enable it. The app also declares these permissions, each for one optional feature:

  • Contacts · only if you turn on "suggest names from contacts". The keyboard then reads names from your address book to offer them as suggestions. Nothing is copied or sent anywhere, and the feature is off by default.
  • User dictionary · to read and add words in Android's shared personal dictionary, if you use it.
  • Vibrate · haptic feedback on key press.
  • Run at startup · so the keyboard is ready after a reboot.

Third parties

No third-party services receive data from the app. Google Play may collect installation and crash statistics under Google's own policies, which are outside our control.

Children

The app is not directed at children and collects no personal information from anyone.

Changes and contact

If this policy changes, the date above will change and the new text will be published here. Questions: hello@curmudgeon.works.
